# Rubrics

Score each criterion 1–5. The anchors define 1, 3 and 5; 2 and 4 sit between them.

## Argument: papers, essays, theses, white papers

| Criterion | 1 | 3 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **thesis_clarity** | You can't say in one sentence what's being claimed. | The claim is clear but hedged, or shifts between sections. | One sharp, falsifiable sentence, held consistently throughout. |
| **originality** | Restates prior art without adding to it. | Adds a real angle to known work, and says what's new. | A genuinely new contribution, clearly positioned against the prior art. |
| **significance** | Low stakes even if true. | Meaningful for a specific field or group. | High stakes for many people, argued convincingly. |
| **evidence** | Claims unsourced or undated; citations missing or suspect. | Main claims sourced; some gaps or overreach. | Every important claim is sourced, dated and proportionate; the evidence against is included. |
| **consistency** | Sections contradict each other. | Minor tensions, not addressed. | Everything hangs together; tensions are named and resolved. |
| **assumptions** | Load-bearing assumptions are hidden. | Listed, but vague about what would break them. | Explicit, with what would break each and a confidence level. |
| **objections** | Strawmen, or no objections at all. | The main objections are present but answered weakly. | The strongest objections are steelmanned, and conceded where they win. |
| **falsifiability** | Nothing could show it's wrong. | Some signposts, but vague ones. | Concrete signposts that would clearly confirm or refute it. |
| **actionability** | No next step. | Next steps listed, but generic. | Cheap, specific next steps that would reduce the biggest uncertainty. |

## Proposal: ventures, products, projects, grant pitches

| Criterion | 1 | 3 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **need** | The problem is asserted, not shown. | Some evidence of the problem and who has it. | Clear evidence of who has it, what it costs them today, and what they use instead. |
| **originality** | It already exists, or it's the obvious move. | A known approach with a real twist, or an underserved niche. | Nobody is doing this, for a reason that no longer holds. |
| **significance** | Few people care even if it works. | Matters to a specific market or community. | Changes outcomes for many people, or opens a large market. |
| **plausibility** | Depends on something false or wildly speculative. | Workable, with one or two big unknowns. | The mechanism is sound, and existing evidence already points its way. |
| **economics** | No way it pays for or sustains itself. | A plausible model, with unproven numbers. | Unit economics or funding are sketched and believable, including the first customers or backers. |
| **risks** | Risks are ignored, including who could be harmed. | The main risks are named, with thin mitigations. | The key risks, and who bears them, are named with credible mitigations or kill criteria. |
| **evidence** | Unsourced claims about markets or behaviour. | The main claims are sourced. | Sourced, dated, and includes evidence against. |
| **actionability** | No way to start. | A clear but costly first step. | A cheap test of the riskiest assumption, runnable within weeks. |

## Plan: design docs, RFCs, project and strategy plans

| Criterion | 1 | 3 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **problem_clarity** | It's unclear what problem is solved, or for whom. | The problem is stated but not bounded (no goals and non-goals). | The problem, goals, non-goals and success measures are explicit. |
| **correctness** | The approach won't work as described. | It works on the happy path; edge cases are unclear. | Sound, including failure modes and edge cases. |
| **alternatives** | None considered. | Alternatives listed, but dismissed without reasons. | Real alternatives compared on the criteria that matter, with the choice justified. |
| **risks** | No risks or failure modes discussed. | Main risks named, with thin mitigations. | Risks, blast radius, rollback and monitoring are covered. |
| **feasibility** | Cost, effort or dependencies are ignored or unrealistic. | Rough estimates, with some dependencies unaddressed. | Realistic effort, cost and dependencies, with the unknowns flagged. |
| **reversibility** | One-way doors are taken without acknowledgement. | Irreversible steps are noted. | It's staged so the irreversible steps come last, after evidence. |
| **measurability** | No way to tell whether it worked. | Metrics are named, without targets. | Metrics, targets and a review point are defined. |

## Hard fails

Any one of these means **reject**, or **revise** if it's easily fixed, whatever the scores:

- **fabricated-citation**: a reference doesn't exist, or doesn't say what's claimed.
- **fatal-flaw**: the core claim or approach rests on something demonstrably false.
- **already-done**: the idea exists and the document doesn't know it, or doesn't say what it adds.
- **harmful**: the idea mainly enables serious harm, or depends on deceiving or exploiting people.
